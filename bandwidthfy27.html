How the numbers work

  • Effort is hands-on time, not calendar time. Figures estimate the time spent producing the work, building the deliverables and running the sessions, not the meetings and coordination around it, and not elapsed weeks.
  • Content-heavy builds carry a larger allowance. The certification paths and eLearnings are weighted up, since that kind of work has historically run past a first estimate.
  • Some lines are rough. A few items are early estimates with little or no time logged yet. Michael's AI Certification excludes L4 (scope still TBD) and Case Management SLA is a rough figure. On Jeffrey's tab, lines marked est are awaiting his confirmation.
  • Everything is editable. Sliders and input fields are adjustable estimates. Change any of them and the reads update live, so the picture can be pressure-tested with your own assumptions.

What was already scoped out

This plate is what remains after deliberate triage. All 34 FY27 Client Ops initiatives were reviewed, and Internal Enablement was scoped out of roughly two-thirds of them, some because there was no real enablement role, several because the role was handed to the team that owned the work.

The largest single reduction was FTV. David's team originally looked to Enablement across several FTV workstreams, and scoping narrowed that to the two Enablement kept, handing the rest back, including #4 the Customer Advocacy Engine and #5 the Sales & CS Product Loop. Combined with other initiatives Enablement was named on but does not own, #10's Help Center content and the deflection-rate content (#24), the corrections took an estimated 35 to 45 hours off the plate, much of it in the same November to December crunch. That relief is already banked.

Recommendation: take on the AI Champions program in October, when L2 wraps, and build it in November

The program adds about 14 hours, and there is room to take it on now rather than parking the build in the January lull. Discovery starts in October once L2 is built, and the build runs in November.

Concentrating the build in November pushes that month to about 73%, the busiest point of the year, but still short of full. One caveat the meters can't capture: taking on the program is also a standing management commitment to roughly 20 champions, continuous, not a one-time build. That ongoing load isn't in the ~14 hours and should be weighed alongside the build fit.

What taking it on involves

The program work, what "build" refers to above, is five workstreams, none of them large. The plan leads with discovery and builds the rest once the fall crunch clears.

  • Discovery with Champions: learn where the program helps, where it gets in the way, and turn that into a short list of adjustments.
  • Improve tracking: build one agent that keeps the Champions' records in Linear and the Notion page current and nudges them weekly on anything missing. This is the heaviest piece.
  • Model and reasoning governance: get Champions logging which model and reasoning level they use per build, and extend that same agent to check those choices against Jacob's governance model and flag recommendations.
  • Leadership reporting: a lightweight quarterly report on the program's value, assembled from work that already exists rather than new data collection.
  • A better Champions meeting: redesign the meeting format so the time is genuinely useful, based on what discovery surfaces.

What it adds up to

The expansion doesn't add load

Both roles land at roughly the same weekly commitment. Trading one Table Read for dedicated trainings across three teams reshuffles the work more than it grows it, because the new team-specific work is modest and the general monthly wrap shrinks as those teams peel off to their own content. If that surprises you, the three-team build estimates are the likeliest place they run low.

Meetings were the missing piece

Adding meetings closes most of the gap this view kept flagging. A calendar sample puts Jeffrey at roughly 6 hours of meetings a week (1:1s, syncs, All-Hands, planning), separate from shrinkage, which lifts the committed load to about 60% of the working week. What's left is a mix of the undercount noted below and genuine room. Real work on the plate is what tells you which.

These are Jeffrey's numbers, and they hold together

The current-role figures are Jeffrey's own, and the Table Read breakdown lands right on the ~4 h/week the v2.5 time-savings doc used for the same work, a good sign they're trustworthy. The few new-role lines marked est are still estimates awaiting Jeffrey's input.

Staying current on the product is the swing factor

At 4 h/week it's the biggest task line after meetings, and it carries into both roles unchanged since it doesn't scale with team count. If that number is soft, the whole picture moves with it, so it's the first one worth pressure-testing.

Read these as heads-down hours

They're focused task times, not elapsed calendar time. Real weeks carry interruptions and context-switching these don't capture, so true utilization is probably somewhat higher than the percentages show. Not near full, though. The gap is too wide for that to be the whole story.

The combined takeaways, and what to do next.

The team read

The team is well deployed, with the two plates full in different ways.

Michael is fully deployed with a deliberate buffer. His committed build runs in the ~40 to 73% band across the fiscal year, front and back loaded, with August to September and November to December busy and January to February light. It looks like there is room, but the visible headroom is held for demand that isn't scoped yet: two initiatives in discovery, live requests in flight, the ad hoc stream, and the risk that the content-heavy fall builds run long. There is no genuinely free time to assign, and new net-new work should trade against something already on the plate.

Jeffrey's move into the role ahead reshuffles work more than it grows it. Both the current role and the role ahead land near 60% of a working week once meetings are counted, so the expansion to three teams is close to load-neutral. The room that's left is real, and the swing factor to watch is staying current on the product, the biggest line after meetings and the one that doesn't scale with team count.
