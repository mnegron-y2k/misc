Section 01 · The problem

Our Support team handles thousands of interactions every month. Reading quality reliably at that scale is genuinely hard.

Our previous QAP efforts fell short structurally.

Gap 01
Coverage was thin
Manual review only touched a fraction of cases. Most work went unseen.
Gap 02
Feedback was late
Manual review was so labor-intensive that coaching arrived weeks or months after the interaction.
Gap 03
Coaching was inconsistent
No shared standard for what "good" looked like. Coaching drifted case to case.
Gap 04
Connor was grading, not coaching
His hours went to scoring cases rather than developing the team.
Gap 05
Compliance signals were invisible
Verification misses, SOP drift, and other high-stakes patterns weren't surfaced until harm had already happened.
Section 02 · The answer

A Quality Assurance Program is the right answer for this shape of problem.

QAPs have been standard in support for decades. The idea is simple: define what good looks like, score cases against it, turn scores into coaching, and let the loop compound. What varies dramatically between programs is execution. How much gets reviewed, how consistent the scoring is, how quickly feedback reaches reps, and whether coaching actually changes behavior.

A good QAP loop looks something like this:

1
Review
Cases scored against a defined rubric.
2
Learn
Patterns and gaps surface across the team.
3
Coach
Feedback turns patterns into concrete moves.
4
Improve
Better performance shows up in the next round.
Section 03 · Common failures

Most QAPs fail in the same five ways.

01

They sample instead of covering everything.

Manual review is expensive, so most programs only score a small percentage of cases. Reps whose cases aren't picked go unseen.

02

They can't sustain the manual work.

Even programs that start comprehensive tend to erode. Managers get pulled elsewhere and the QAP becomes something that happens only when someone remembers.

03

They score inconsistently.

When scoring depends on human judgment without shared anchors, the same case gets scored differently by different reviewers. Reps notice and lose trust in the signal.

04

They coach generically.

"You need to improve your communication" doesn't help anyone change. Without specific evidence tied to specific moments, coaching becomes noise reps learn to nod through.

05

They pick a side on AI or humans.

Pure-AI programs feel hollow and can hallucinate. Pure-human programs don't scale. The programs that work use AI for what it does well and human judgment for what only humans can do.

These are the five failure modes our program was designed from day one to avoid.
Section 04 · Our design

Our QAP runs as a three-phase monthly cycle, each phase designed against a specific failure.

Phase 01
Loading
Every closed case from the month gets pulled into one place automatically. Email, chat, and phone.
Prevents
Thin coverage. Manual work erosion.
Phase 02
Scoring
Every case evaluated against one rubric with shared anchors, calibrated to Connor's judgment.
Prevents
Inconsistent scoring across reviewers and time.
Phase 03
Delivery
Scores become custom coaching per rep, drawn from real evidence and Wrapbook-specific model behaviors.
Prevents
Generic coaching. Feedback that's too late to matter.
The fifth failure — picking a side on AI or humans — is handled across the whole cycle. AI does what AI does well. Connor does what only Connor can do.
Section 05 · Deep Dive
01
Loading

How the Loading phase works.

What is the phase?
Loading is the first step of the cycle. It brings every closed case from the prior month into one scoreable place, regardless of which channel it came through.
When does it occur?
At the start of each monthly cycle.
How does it occur?

Our QAP scores cases across phone, live chat, and email. Internal systems are already orchestrated to capture most of this, so email and chat load automatically from Salesforce into the Case Scores Log.

Phone works differently. For calls, we built QAPIntake, an agent that parses a Zoom Contact Center CSV export, matches each call to its Salesforce case, and populates the Case Scores Log. This part is manual for now: each month, Connor exports the CSV once and drops it in QAPIntake.

Scoring is the second step. Every loaded case gets evaluated against our rubric, producing a full record of what happened and how well it went.

Section 06 · Deep Dive · Continued
02
Scoring

How do we know it's accurate?

To make sure QAPScorer scores accurately, we did three things:

1
Built a rubric.
Clear scoring criteria with anchors for each of the 13 skills. Without shared anchors, scoring drifts case to case. With them, the same case scored twice produces the same result.
2
Built the Coaching Library.
A Notion knowledge base with an entry for each of the 13 skills. Each entry defines what "good" looks like at Wrapbook Support specifically: model phrases top performers use, common pitfalls, and seven rotating coaching angles. Without this, QAPScorer would score against generic customer-service ideas. With it, we score against what our reps should be doing in our context. The same library also powers coaching in Phase 3.

Section 07 · Deep Dive
03
Delivery

How the Delivery phase works.

What is the phase?
Delivery is the final step. All scoring is done, so QAPDigest takes the raw scores and spins them into personalized coaching each rep will respond to, plus a team summary for the manager.
When does it occur?
After scoring completes and Connor confirms calibration.
How does it occur?
Connor triggers QAPDigest in Dust. For each rep, it drafts a coaching digest in a six-block structure per skill area: strengths from the cycle, the largest opportunity, model behavior to work toward, what continuing the current pattern looks like, a measurable next-cycle behavior, and a mentor-style closing on why this matters for their work. Coaching pulls from two distinct sources depending on what's being coached (detailed below). Connor reviews every draft in the Digest Log; approved digests fire as Slack DMs.
For Product Knowledge
Grounded in the SOP itself.
Coaching comes from our documented processes, not general knowledge.

QAPDigest pulls the model behavior from the current SOP for that specific skill. It quotes what the rep actually did, then points to the exact places to learn more: the SOP page in Notion, the Wrapbook Help Center article, the relevant WorkRamp course.

Every reference is a fresh search each cycle, so nothing goes stale.

Result: reps see exactly what to do and where to learn more.
For Soft Skills
Grounded in real customer moments.
Coaching comes from what actually happened, not what should have happened in theory.

QAPDigest quotes the rep's exact language, quotes how the customer responded, and pulls model phrases the top performers on the team use in similar moments (anonymized).

Coaching ties directly to real Wrapbook situations: high-emotion payment issues, time-sensitive union deadlines, first-time production coordinators, line producers under financial pressure.

Result: reps learn the principle behind the behavior, not just the rule.
Connor reviews every draft before anything reaches a rep. Once approved, the digest fires as a Slack DM.

Product Knowledge
Where you're doing excellent
Sam, you showed strong payment-reissue workflow knowledge in case 974301, where you wrote:
"I have reissued them to direct deposit."
You had already reviewed the payment history, confirmed the direct-deposit update, and completed the correct void-and-reissue path. That combination matters when a production coordinator is waiting on payroll to move and needs a clear answer rather than another handoff.
Your largest opportunity right now
The forward-looking product knowledge opportunity is making the reissue dependency and timing equally specific. In case 974092, you wrote:
"Once confirmed, we will proceed with the reissue right away."
The dependency is clear, but "right away" leaves the customer without a usable window.
Library: Managing Expectations
What good looks like here
Keep the same accurate precondition and add a customer-facing window:
"Here's what to expect: once the banking update is confirmed, I'll submit the reissue by [specific time]. If anything changes, I'll message you before then."
Reference: 📋 Check Returns (SOP) · Request a void and reissue of a worker payment (Help Center)
What keeping the current pattern looks like
The work can be technically correct while the customer still has to chase for timing. A line producer under financial pressure may update a worker's bank information, hear "right away," and then have no clear point when the reissue should appear or when Support will update them.
How we'll know it's sticking
In the next cycle, every substantive payment-reissue reply should include the required dependency, a specific time or window for the next action, and a proactive-update trigger if that window changes.
Why this matters for your work
You already do the hard product work of finding the payment history and applying the correct reissue path. Adding a precise customer-time commitment will make that expertise easier to trust during payroll-critical moments, especially when a production team is working toward a shoot or union deadline.
Soft Skills
Where you're doing excellent
You consistently stayed with customers through complicated fixes and closed with a real outcome. In case 974301, you wrote "I have reissued them to direct deposit," and the customer replied:
"Thank you so much for your help."
The customer's response shows the action landed clearly and your ownership carried through to resolution.
Your largest opportunity right now
The useful stretch is turning a successful fix into a more explicit three-part close on phone cases. On the Aug 20 phone case, you ended with:
"if you have any more problems, feel free to give us a call back."
That is warm, but broad.
Library: Confirming Understanding & Closing
What good looks like here
Use the library's three-part move:
"To recap: we reset the password and completed MFA, you're back in the account, and if the login issue returns, call us back. Sound good?"
Another team member modeled the specific reopen invite:
"let me know, though, if anything goes wrong with that check ... glad to see that you got it and everything's gonna work out."
What keeping the current pattern looks like
A broad "call us back" close can leave a customer unsure whether Support is still watching the issue or whether they must start over. In a time-sensitive payment case, that ambiguity can turn a correct fix into a reopen.
How we'll know it's sticking
In the next cycle, use a three-part recap plus one direct confirmation question in every substantive phone interaction that reaches a fix. The review check is simple: the call ending contains the action taken, the next step or timing, and the specific trigger for coming back.
Why this matters for your work
Your phone work shows patience, accurate diagnosis, and steady ownership. A more deliberate close will help customers leave a payment, onboarding, or access call knowing exactly what changed and what to do next, which makes your strong resolution work easier for the customer to carry forward.

Section 11 · How we got here

This wasn't the first version.

A phased rollout, tested against reality at each step. Every phase had to earn the next.

01 · Complete
Calibration
Shadow mode for a month. QAPScorer scored 68 cases while Connor scored the same 68 in parallel. 3 iterations of anchor refinement. Ended with 63 of 68 matching Connor's judgment.
02 · Complete
Shadow delivery
QAPDigest drafted real coaching digests but they weren't sent to reps. Connor and I reviewed drafts side-by-side to catch tone issues, factual gaps, and structural problems. Fixed the biggest ones before any real coaching reached anyone.
03 · Now · Sept 17
Supervised (Phase 2)
Real digests flow to reps via Slack. Connor reviews every draft in the Digest Log before it's sent. Full human control, agent doing the heavy drafting. This is where we are today.
04 · Target · November
Fully autonomous (Phase 3)
Approved digest structure ships without per-digest review. Connor shifts to spot-checking cases and maintaining the Coaching Library. Contingent on the Nov 2 go/no-go decision.
